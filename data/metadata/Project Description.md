Case Study 5: Project "Lullaby" 

Target Population: Low-Income Postpartum Mothers with Pregnancy-Induced Hypertension  (PIH) 

Geographical setting: South Phoenix and Mesa (Urban Safety-Net Catchment Areas) 

Study setting options: Obstetrics clinic, safety net healthcare system, FQHC, Mom’s support  groups 

Clinical Context: 

Preeclampsia and pregnancy-induced hypertension often persist or worsen after delivery. The  12-week postpartum period is highly dangerous because hospital monitoring stops, but the risk  of stroke remains. Early warning signs (headaches, elevated blood pressure, severe swelling)  require immediate clinical intervention. 

The Lived Experience: 

These mothers are experiencing brutal sleep deprivation, painful physical recovery, and  massive hormonal shifts. In the Phoenix area, low-income families frequently face "energy  poverty"—the inability to afford adequate air conditioning during the 4-to-5 month summer. The  chronic physiological stress of heat exposure exacerbates hypertension and causes additional  swelling, making it difficult to distinguish between environmental heat strain and an impending  cardiovascular crisis. Many lack paid maternity leave, making self-care an impossible luxury. 

Digital & Environmental Constraints: 

Smartphones are constantly at their side, but they are already overwhelmed by the cognitive  burden of tracking infant data. Asking them to manually interact with a complex new interface to  log their own physiological data will result in immediate abandonment. 

The Mandate: 

Develop a digital health intervention to identify dangerous cardiovascular events and prevent  maternal mortality during the critical 12-week postpartum period. You must select the specific  data collection technologies and behavioral strategies used, explicitly justifying how your  proposed system overcomes sleep deprivation, heat-induced physiological stress, and cognitive  overload without adding to the mother's anxiety.
